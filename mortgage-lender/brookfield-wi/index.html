How do you choose a mortgage lender in Brookfield?

Choose a lender that is comfortable with larger loan amounts, can structure a purchase before your current home sells, and turns appraisals around quickly. Brookfield listings with good schools and big lots draw serious buyers, and sellers notice whose financing looks solid.

I'd ask any lender you interview three Brookfield-specific questions: How do you handle a loan just above the conforming limit? Can I buy before I sell, and what will that take? And how fast can you get an appraiser into the house? My answers come out of a customized buyer consultation, because the right structure depends on your equity, income and timeline.

When does a Brookfield purchase need a jumbo loan?

You need a jumbo loan when the loan amount — not the price — is above FHFA's 2026 conforming limit of $832,750 for a single-family home in Waukesha County.

That distinction matters in Brookfield. A buyer bringing substantial equity from a previous home may keep the loan under the limit on a higher-priced house, while a similar buyer with less equity crosses it. Jumbo loans aren't harder for well-qualified buyers, but they usually look more closely at reserves, documentation and the appraisal. I walk through the specifics in jumbo loans in Wisconsin.

Below the limit, a conventional loan is the usual fit. HUD's 2026 FHA limit in Waukesha County is $541,287, which still covers many Brookfield condos and smaller ranches.

Can you buy a Brookfield home before selling your current one?

Often, yes — and it usually wins you the house. A home sale contingency makes your offer weaker in a competitive Brookfield listing, so many move-up buyers look for ways to buy first.

  • Qualify carrying both payments. If income supports both homes for a short overlap, no contingency is needed.
  • Tap your existing equity to fund the down payment on the new home.
  • Write a stronger offer with a fully underwritten pre-approval and a short, reliable financing timeline.

Each option has trade-offs, and I lay them out in how to buy before you sell.

Can Brookfield buyers use down payment assistance?

Yes, though it fits a narrower slice of Brookfield than other suburbs. All cities in Waukesha County, including Brookfield, are eligible for the HOME Consortium's five-year forgivable down payment loan, and Fairway is on the consortium's participating-lender list.

The limits are what narrow it: household income must be at or below 80 percent of the county median ($86,700 for a family of four, effective June 1, 2026), and the home's value can't exceed $390,000 for an existing home or $440,000 for new construction in Waukesha County. In Brookfield, that tends to mean condos and smaller older homes. Check current funding availability before relying on it, and see my Waukesha page for the full program rules.

FAQ

Questions I hear most.

What is the 2026 jumbo loan threshold in Brookfield?
Any loan amount above $832,750 on a single-family home is a jumbo loan in 2026, because that's FHFA's conforming limit for Waukesha County. The threshold applies to the loan, not the purchase price, so a larger down payment from your current home's equity can keep a higher-priced Brookfield purchase in conforming territory.
Do I need to sell my Brookfield home before buying another?
Not necessarily. If your income supports both housing costs for a short overlap, or you can use existing equity toward the new down payment, you may be able to buy first and skip a home sale contingency. The right approach depends on your equity, debts and how quickly your current home is likely to sell.
Is down payment assistance available in Brookfield?
Brookfield is inside the Waukesha County HOME Consortium's eligible area, so income-qualified buyers can use its forgivable down payment loan. The 2026 house value cap for Waukesha County is $390,000 for existing homes, which in Brookfield mostly means condos and smaller homes. Funding is set annually, so confirm availability first.
Are jumbo loans harder to get than conventional loans?
For well-qualified buyers, not dramatically. Jumbo loans typically ask for more cash reserves after closing, fuller documentation of income and assets, and sometimes closer appraisal review. Planning ahead matters more than anything, so I review jumbo files before you start touring rather than after you have an accepted offer.
How quickly can you close on a Brookfield home?
My team can close in as few as 9 to 18 days when the file, appraisal and title are ready, and appraisals have come back in as little as three to four business days. Jumbo files and buy-before-you-sell structures may need more lead time, so we set the contract's financing deadline around your specific plan.
