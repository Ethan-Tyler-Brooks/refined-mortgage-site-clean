What should West Allis buyers look for in a mortgage lender?

Look for a lender who's comfortable with two-unit properties, older-home repair conditions and layered assistance, because that's what West Allis purchases tend to involve.

A lot of buyers here are first-timers, and many are weighing a duplex they can live in while a tenant helps with costs. That's a different underwriting conversation than a single-family home, and it's worth having before you tour. My team starts with a customized buyer consultation, then keeps you and your agent updated at every milestone.

What kinds of homes will you find in West Allis?

West Allis is mostly early- and mid-20th-century housing: brick and frame bungalows, Cape Cods, modest ranches and a large stock of upper/lower duplexes on compact city lots.

The city grew up around industry and is home to Wisconsin State Fair Park, and its neighborhoods have the walkable grid that comes with that history. For buyers, older stock means inspections deserve attention: roofs, sewer laterals, electrical updates and lead paint on pre-1978 homes are common negotiation points. If you're new to the process, my first-time buyer mistakes post covers the traps that cost people houses.

Should you buy a duplex in West Allis?

A West Allis duplex can be a smart first purchase if you'll live in one unit and you're ready to be a landlord. Owner-occupied two-unit homes qualify for FHA and conventional financing, and projected rent from the other unit may help you qualify, within each program's rules.

2026 limits leave room: HUD's FHA limit for a two-unit home in Milwaukee County is $693,050, and FHFA's conforming limit for two units is $1,066,250. Before you write an offer, have your lender run the numbers with a realistic rent estimate and repair reserve. More in buying a duplex in Wisconsin, and see the FHA loan page for program basics.

Does West Allis offer homebuyer or home repair help?

Yes, mainly on the repair side. In June 2026 the City of West Allis announced two rehab loan programs for homeowners:

  • Housing Rehabilitation Loan Program — up to $40,000 for owner-occupied single-family homes and duplexes, with income limits.
  • TID Residential Rehabilitation Loan Program — up to $25,000 for owner-occupied single-family homes in targeted areas, with no income restrictions.

The city's Office of Housing also lists a Home Buyer Loan Program; call the city to confirm current terms and funding availability before you plan around it.

Because West Allis is in Milwaukee County, income-eligible buyers can also ask a HUD-approved counseling agency about state-funded HCRI grants and the WORTH grant for first-time buyers purchasing in Milwaukee County, and look at statewide WHEDA programs.

Is a renovation loan a good fit for a West Allis fixer-upper?

It can be, when a house needs more work than you can fund from savings after closing. An FHA 203(k) loan wraps the purchase and approved repairs into one mortgage, based on the home's value after the work is done.

The trade-off is time and paperwork: contractor bids, a repair scope and inspections during the work. For lighter projects, buying a move-in-ready home and using a city rehab loan later may be simpler. Compare both in FHA 203(k) renovation loans in Wisconsin.

How do property taxes and closing costs work in West Allis?

West Allis follows Wisconsin's standard schedule: taxes are billed in December and paid in arrears, either in full by January 31 or in two installments due January 31 and July 31. At closing, the seller credits you for the part of the year they owned the home.

The seller pays the state transfer fee of $3 per $1,000 of value. You'll cover lender, appraisal and title charges plus prepaid escrow. See how much cash you need at closing before you set your budget.

Can I use an FHA loan to buy a West Allis duplex?
Yes, as long as you'll live in one of the units. FHA allows owner-occupied two- to four-unit purchases, and HUD's 2026 FHA limit for a two-unit home in Milwaukee County is $693,050. The appraisal will review the condition of both units, so older duplexes with deferred maintenance may need repairs before closing.
Does West Allis have a first-time homebuyer program?
The City of West Allis Office of Housing lists a Home Buyer Loan Program, but terms and funding change, so call the city to confirm what's available now. Income-eligible buyers can also ask HUD-approved counseling agencies about HCRI grants and the WORTH grant for Milwaukee County first-time buyers, plus WHEDA's statewide programs.
What home repair loans does West Allis offer in 2026?
In June 2026 the city announced a Housing Rehabilitation Loan Program of up to $40,000 for owner-occupied single-family homes and duplexes with income limits, and a TID Residential Rehabilitation Loan Program of up to $25,000 for owner-occupied single-family homes in targeted areas with no income restrictions. Contact the city for current terms.
Is West Allis a good place for a first-time buyer?
Many first-time buyers find it a good fit because the housing stock includes smaller single-family homes and owner-occupied duplexes, and the city offers repair loans for owner-occupants. Whether it fits you depends on your budget, commute and appetite for older-home maintenance, so we start with the numbers and your plans.
