How does Madison's Home-Buy the American Dream program work?

Home-Buy the American Dream (HBAD) is a City of Madison Community Development Division loan that helps first-time buyers cover down payment and closing costs on a home in the city. Per the city's 2026 program materials:

  • Amount: up to $35,000 for a City of Madison property.
  • Structure: a deferred, shared-appreciation loan, repaid when you sell, stop living in the home, or do a cash-out refinance for something other than home improvement.
  • Who qualifies: first-time buyers who haven't owned a home in the last three years, with household income at or below 80 percent of the Dane County median — $106,800 for a family of four under the 2026 limits.
  • Property: a single-family home, condominium or half of a duplex inside the City of Madison, used as your primary residence. Properties outside the city haven't qualified since May 1, 2023.
  • Requirements: homebuyer education and a counseling session with a HUD-approved agency, a Housing Quality Standards inspection, a lead-based paint inspection on pre-1978 homes, and a cap on liquid assets left after closing.

Your lender submits the application to the city, which accepts files for pre-approval. Assistance is first come, first funded, so check current funding availability early.

What other down payment help exists in Dane County?

Dane County has several other programs, summarized on the Home Buyers Round Table's 2026 matrix published by the city:

  • Movin' Out Dane County CDBG — a deferred loan for income-eligible buyers in Dane County outside the City of Madison.
  • Urban League of Greater Madison Progressive UpPayment — assistance for buyers who complete its homebuyer education and counseling.
  • Home Buyers Round Table grant — a small grant with a limited number available.
  • Movin' Out programs for households that include a member with a permanent disability.
  • Downpayment Plus and WHEDA options statewide.

The matrix notes that some programs must sit in second-mortgage position and can't be stacked with each other, while HBAD materials allow combining funds in some cases. We map the rules before you write an offer.

What is Madison's housing market like for buyers?

Madison is a higher-priced, competitive market: HUD's 2026 loan-limit table lists a median sale price of $448,000 for Dane County, roughly even with the $447,000 HUD lists for the Milwaukee metro counties and well above Racine or Kenosha.

Housing ranges from condos downtown and on the isthmus to older homes in near-east and near-west neighborhoods and newer subdivisions on the edges of the city. Condos are a common entry point, so read condo financing in Wisconsin before you fall for a unit. What loan limits and tax rules apply in Dane County?

For 2026, FHFA's conforming limit in Dane County is $832,750 for one unit, and HUD's FHA limit is $541,287. Larger loans move into jumbo territory.

Property taxes follow the statewide schedule: billed in December, paid in arrears, and due in full by January 31 or in two installments due January 31 and July 31. The seller credits your share at closing, and the seller pays the state transfer fee of $3 per $1,000 of value. Once you move in, claim the lottery and gaming credit on your primary residence. FAQ

Questions I hear most.

Who qualifies for Madison's Home-Buy the American Dream program?
Per the City of Madison, first-time buyers who haven't owned a home in three years, with household income at or below 80 percent of the Dane County median, buying a single-family home, condo or half of a duplex in the City of Madison as a primary residence. Homebuyer education, counseling and inspections are required.
How much can Home-Buy the American Dream provide?
The city's 2026 materials list a maximum of $35,000 toward down payment and closing costs for a City of Madison property. It's a deferred, shared-appreciation loan rather than a grant, repaid when you sell, move out, or do a cash-out refinance for purposes other than home improvement. Funding is first come, first funded.
Can I use HBAD for a home outside the City of Madison?
No. Since May 1, 2023, the city has required HBAD properties to be inside City of Madison limits. Buyers elsewhere in Dane County can look at Movin' Out's Dane County CDBG program, Urban League of Greater Madison assistance, Downpayment Plus and WHEDA, each with its own income limits and requirements.
Can a Milwaukee-area lender close a Madison loan?
Yes. Mortgage licensing is statewide, so a Wisconsin-licensed lender can finance a home anywhere in the state. I work from Wauwatosa, a bit over an hour from Madison, and handle most steps by phone, video and secure upload, with local appraisers and title companies handling the on-the-ground pieces.
What loan limits apply in Madison in 2026?
For 2026, the conforming loan limit in Dane County is $832,750 for a single-family home, set by FHFA, and HUD's FHA limit is $541,287. Loans above the conforming limit are jumbo loans, which typically need stronger reserves and fuller documentation, so plan ahead if you're shopping at the top of the market.
