What makes buying a home in Wauwatosa different?

Tosa's housing stock is mostly older and well-kept, demand is steady, and the best houses draw multiple offers — so financing strategy matters as much as price.

You'll see 1920s Tudors and colonials in Washington Highlands, bungalows and Cape Cods throughout the east side, and mid-century ranches farther west toward Mayfair and the county grounds. Older homes bring older-home questions: knob-and-tube wiring, original sewer laterals and lead paint can show up in inspections, and they affect what you'll want to negotiate.

Does Wauwatosa offer down payment assistance?

Not directly right now. The City of Wauwatosa's 2026 CDBG Annual Action Plan says the city hasn't provided homebuyer assistance since 2017, noting that the program's requirements limited its use, and the city instead points buyers to Milwaukee County HOME Consortium first-time homebuyer programs.

Tosa buyers who meet income guidelines can still look at:

  • HCRI grants — state-funded down payment and closing cost grants, available through counseling agencies such as Housing Resources Inc., whose office at 7830 W Burleigh St. is a few miles east of mine on the same street.
  • WORTH grants for first-time buyers purchasing anywhere in Milwaukee County, with separate funds based on income.
  • WHEDA first mortgages and assistance, available statewide. See my WHEDA loan page.

Income limits and funding change, so check current funding availability with a HUD-approved counselor before you write an offer that depends on it.

What loan limits apply in Wauwatosa in 2026?

For 2026, FHFA's conforming loan limit in Milwaukee County is $832,750 for a single-family home, and HUD's FHA limit is $541,287.

Most Tosa purchases fit comfortably inside the conforming limit, which is why many of my clients use a conventional loan. A larger Washington Highlands home financed above the conforming limit needs a jumbo loan, which has its own reserve and documentation standards. FHA can still be a good fit for a first Tosa bungalow, particularly when credit is still being rebuilt.

How do property taxes affect a Wauwatosa purchase?

Wauwatosa property taxes, like everywhere in Wisconsin, are billed in December and paid in arrears, so the seller credits you their share of the current year at closing.

Under state law, a bill can be paid in full by January 31 or in two installments due January 31 and July 31. If you escrow, your lender pays the bill and builds it into your monthly payment; if you don't, put both dates on your calendar. And once you move in, claim the lottery and gaming credit on your primary residence. More in Wisconsin property taxes and escrow.

Is there a Wauwatosa first-time homebuyer program?
Wauwatosa's 2026 CDBG plan says the city hasn't provided homebuyer assistance since 2017 and refers buyers to Milwaukee County HOME Consortium programs instead. Income-eligible buyers can also ask HUD-approved counseling agencies about state HCRI grants and the WORTH grant for Milwaukee County first-time buyers, plus WHEDA's statewide options. Check current funding before you count on any of them.
How competitive is buying a home in Wauwatosa?
Well-priced homes in neighborhoods like the Village, Washington Highlands and the east side often draw multiple offers. A fully underwritten pre-approval, a quick appraisal and a lender who calls the listing agent can make your offer stronger without overpaying. Your agent can tell you how a specific listing is drawing interest.
Do I need a jumbo loan to buy in Wauwatosa?
Only if your loan amount is above the 2026 conforming limit of $832,750 for a single-family home in Milwaukee County. Most Wauwatosa purchases fall below that. Above it, a jumbo loan typically asks for stronger reserves and fuller documentation, so plan earlier if you're shopping at the top of the market.
