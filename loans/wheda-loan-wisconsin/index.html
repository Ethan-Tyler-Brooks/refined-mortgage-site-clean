WHEDA first-time buyer loans

WHEDA Loans for Wisconsin First-Time Buyers

WHEDA, Wisconsin's state housing finance agency, offers special first-time homebuyer pricing on its Advantage Conventional loan and an optional Easy Close second mortgage to help with down payment and closing costs. I pair the two for buyers who fit WHEDA's income and purchase price limits.

What is a WHEDA loan?

A WHEDA loan is a mortgage built on the Wisconsin Housing and Economic Development Authority's guidelines and offered through participating lenders; WHEDA doesn't lend to you directly.

The combination I use is WHEDA's first-time homebuyer product: a WHEDA Advantage Conventional first mortgage with WHEDA's first-time home buyer (FTHB) pricing, which WHEDA reserves for buyers who meet its first-time definition and its county income and purchase price limits. When a buyer needs help with cash to close, I add WHEDA's Easy Close down payment assistance. Both must be for a home you'll live in as your principal residence, and they can be used statewide.

Who counts as a first-time buyer for WHEDA?

WHEDA treats you as a first-time buyer if you haven't had an ownership interest in a principal residence in the last three years, measured up to the date you sign your new loan.

Two Wisconsin wrinkles matter here:

  • Marital property: because Wisconsin is a marital property state, a spouse who isn't on the loan must also meet the definition, and ownership from an earlier marriage within the three years counts.
  • Exceptions: WHEDA waives the first-time requirement for homes in federally designated target areas and for eligible veterans with an honorable discharge.

If you sold a home a few years back, check your closing date; you may already qualify again. My post on buying without your spouse in Wisconsin explains how marital property affects a purchase.

What are WHEDA's income and purchase price limits?

WHEDA sets income limits and purchase price limits by county, and the current limits took effect June 13, 2026.

  • Income limits vary by county and household size, with one figure for 1 to 2 people and a higher one for 3 or more.
  • Purchase price limits vary by county.
  • Target areas have higher limits in some cases, alongside the first-time buyer waiver.

WHEDA measures what it calls total household compliance income, which can differ from the income a lender uses to qualify you. I check your county's limits against your household before we go further, because being just over the line changes the plan.

How does WHEDA Easy Close down payment assistance work?

Easy Close is a second mortgage from WHEDA that can cover your down payment, closing costs and prepaid items, recorded behind your WHEDA first mortgage.

  • The amount is capped at a share of the purchase price or appraised value, whichever is lower.
  • It's a fully amortizing fixed-rate loan with its own monthly payment, starting the same month as your first mortgage, and it's paid off over a shorter term.
  • It must be paired with a WHEDA Advantage first mortgage.

Easy Close is a loan, not a grant, so it adds to your monthly payment. For many buyers that trade is worth it to keep savings intact. See how much cash you need at closing in Wisconsin to size the gap first.

What homebuyer education does WHEDA require?

WHEDA requires pre-purchase homebuyer education when you're a first-time buyer, and at least one borrower signing the loan must complete it.

Approved options include Fannie Mae's online courses, such as HomeView, and HUD-approved housing counseling agencies. Your certificate is good for twelve months from completion, so I tell buyers to finish it while they're still house hunting rather than after an offer is accepted. If one co-borrower isn't a first-time buyer, the requirement can be waived.

Is WHEDA the right choice for me?

WHEDA is a strong fit when you're a first-time buyer under the county limits and want help with cash to close, but it isn't automatically the cheapest option.

I compare it against FHA, Fannie Mae HomeReady and local city programs on the same house. My WHEDA down payment assistance post covers repayment basics, and my first-time homebuyer guide walks through every option and step. To see all programs at once, visit the loan programs hub.

FAQ

Questions I hear most.

Do I have to be a first-time buyer to use WHEDA?
For the first-time homebuyer product I use, yes. You can't have had an ownership interest in a principal residence in the last three years. WHEDA waives that requirement for homes in federally designated target areas and for eligible veterans with an honorable discharge. Income and purchase price limits for your county still apply either way.
Is WHEDA Easy Close free money?
No. Easy Close is a second mortgage with its own monthly payment, a fixed rate and a shorter repayment term than your first mortgage. It can cover down payment, closing costs and prepaid items, which keeps more of your savings in the bank. I show you the combined payment with and without it so you can decide whether the trade is worth it.
What are the WHEDA income limits in Milwaukee County?
WHEDA publishes income and purchase price limits for every Wisconsin county, with the current figures effective June 13, 2026. Income limits depend on household size, with separate figures for 1 to 2 people and 3 or more, and federally designated target areas have their own, separate limits. I check your household against the current table before we start.
How long does WHEDA homebuyer education take?
It depends on the course you choose. Fannie Mae's HomeView is a free online course, and HUD-approved housing counseling agencies offer classes and one-on-one counseling. Only one borrower signing the loan needs to complete it, and the certificate stays valid for twelve months, so finishing it early keeps it from delaying your closing.
Can I use WHEDA for a duplex or condo?
WHEDA's conventional program allows single-family homes, condominiums, manufactured homes and 2 to 4 unit properties, as long as you live in the home. Multi-unit purchases come with extra requirements, including landlord education and borrower funds on certain property types. Condos also have to meet project eligibility rules, so I review the specific property before you make an offer.
