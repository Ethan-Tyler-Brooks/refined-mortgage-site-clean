USDA Loans in Wisconsin

A USDA loan is a mortgage guaranteed by USDA Rural Development for moderate-income households buying a primary home in an eligible rural area. Plenty of Wisconsin towns within commuting distance of Milwaukee are on USDA's eligible map.

What is a USDA loan?

A USDA loan, formally the Single Family Housing Guaranteed Loan Program, is a mortgage from an approved lender that USDA Rural Development guarantees, which lets the lender offer flexible terms in eligible rural areas.

It has three gates: the property must sit in an eligible area, your household income must fall under the county limit, and you must live in the home as your primary residence. Borrowers must be U.S. citizens, U.S. non-citizen nationals or qualified aliens. USDA sets no maximum purchase price; what you can borrow comes down to income and debts. For buyers who qualify, it offers some of the most flexible down payment rules available.

Which areas near Milwaukee are USDA-eligible?

Milwaukee County and the built-up suburbs around it are not USDA-eligible, but many smaller communities a short drive out are.

When I checked USDA's eligibility map in October 2026:

  • Eligible: addresses in and around Mukwonago, East Troy, Waterford, Union Grove, Jackson, Kewaskum, Twin Lakes and Paddock Lake.
  • Not eligible: Waukesha, Brookfield, Menomonee Falls, Franklin, Cedarburg and Grafton, plus the larger outlying cities of West Bend, Oconomowoc, Hartford, Burlington, Watertown and Port Washington. Country addresses just outside those cities can still qualify.

USDA updates the map, and the boundary can split a neighborhood, so I check the exact address on USDA's eligibility site before you write an offer.

How do USDA income limits work?

USDA caps total household income at a moderate-income limit set for each county and household size, and it counts every adult in the household, not just the people on the loan.

That trips people up. An adult child or parent living with you who has a job counts toward the limit even if they're not borrowing. USDA does allow deductions that lower your adjusted income, including amounts for each dependent, for household members age 62 or older and for verified childcare costs for children under 12. I run the numbers on USDA's income tool before we go further, because a household that looks over the line can sometimes fit once deductions apply.

What does a USDA loan cost instead of mortgage insurance?

USDA loans carry a guarantee fee instead of private mortgage insurance: an upfront fee that can be financed into the loan and an annual fee spread into your monthly payment.

Interest rates are set by each lender, not USDA, so it pays to compare. You'll still have regular closing costs; my post on how much cash you need at closing in Wisconsin breaks those down, and seller-paid closing costs explains how sellers can contribute.

What kinds of homes qualify?

Most owner-occupied homes qualify if they're in an eligible area and in sound condition.

  • Existing single-family homes and new construction
  • Townhomes and condos, with some limitations
  • Modular homes, and manufactured homes that meet USDA's age and other rules

You must move in within 60 days of closing and keep living there. Personal gardens and hobby farms are allowed, but land or buildings used mainly to produce income are not, so I review any acreage listing early.

Is USDA better than FHA, VA or WHEDA?

USDA is often the strongest option for a moderate-income buyer purchasing in an eligible area, but it isn't automatically the cheapest.

Veterans should compare it with a VA loan, which has no income cap. Buyers in Milwaukee County, where USDA isn't available, usually land on FHA or WHEDA. My USDA Rural Development post has a worked example, and the loan programs hub lines everything up side by side.

FAQ

Questions I hear most.

Is Waukesha County eligible for USDA loans?
Parts of it. When I checked USDA's map in October 2026, the cities of Waukesha, Brookfield, Menomonee Falls and Oconomowoc were not eligible, but addresses in and around Mukwonago were. Eligibility can change between neighboring streets and USDA updates the map, so the only reliable answer is checking the specific address, which I do before you make an offer.
Who counts toward the USDA household income limit?
Every adult member of the household counts, not just the people signing the loan. That includes an adult child or parent who lives with you and earns income. USDA then allows deductions, such as amounts for dependents, household members age 62 or older and childcare for children under 12, which can bring adjusted income under the county limit.
Do I have to be a first-time buyer for a USDA loan?
No. USDA doesn't require you to be a first-time buyer. You do need to live in the home as your primary residence, meet the household income limit and buy in an eligible area. If you already own a home, I'll look at how that property fits into your plans, since USDA is meant for the home you'll actually live in.
Is there a maximum purchase price for a USDA loan?
No. USDA's guaranteed program has no maximum purchase price. The limits come from the household income cap and your ability to qualify for the payment, which is based on income, debts and credit. USDA doesn't publish a minimum credit score, but credit guidelines vary by lender. I'll tell you exactly where you stand after a quick review.
How long does a USDA loan take to close?
USDA loans include an extra step: after the lender underwrites the file, it requests a conditional commitment from USDA. That can add time compared with conventional or FHA, so planning matters. I build USDA's review into the contract timeline with your agent, order the appraisal early and keep you updated at each milestone so there are no surprises close to closing day.
