Wisconsin Home Loan Programs, Compared

Most Wisconsin buyers end up choosing between five programs: conventional, FHA, VA, USDA and WHEDA. The right one depends on your service history, where the home is, your household income and how much cash you want to keep in the bank.

Which Wisconsin home loan program fits you?

The program that fits you is usually decided by four questions: Have you served in the military? Is the home in a USDA-eligible area? Is your household income under a program limit? And would you rather keep cash in savings or pay less over time?

I walk every client through those questions before we ever talk about a pre-approval amount, because the answers narrow the field fast. A veteran buying in Wauwatosa, a first-time buyer in Racine and a family moving out to Mukwonago can all be well served by completely different loans. Which program costs less over time?

The cheapest program is the one whose fees and mortgage insurance you'll actually carry the shortest time, so how long you plan to keep the loan matters as much as the program itself.

  • Conventional PMI can come off once you build enough equity, which often makes it cheaper for buyers with stronger credit.
  • FHA MIP usually stays for the life of the loan with a smaller down payment, but FHA can still win when credit is thinner.
  • VA and USDA swap monthly mortgage insurance for a funding or guarantee fee.

I run the programs you qualify for side by side, on the same house, before you choose. My post on conventional vs. FHA in Wisconsin shows how that comparison works, and whether you really need a big down payment explains why putting less down is often the smarter move.

Are there Wisconsin programs just for first-time buyers?

Yes. WHEDA, Wisconsin's state housing finance agency, offers first-time homebuyer pricing on its conventional loan and an optional down payment assistance second mortgage called Easy Close.

Beyond WHEDA, Fannie Mae's HomeReady and Freddie Mac's Home Possible serve buyers whose income falls under the local area median limit, and several Wisconsin cities run their own assistance programs. My first-time homebuyer guide lays out the 3-year rule and the step-by-step process, and my WHEDA down payment assistance post covers the basics of how assistance gets repaid.

What if my situation doesn't fit a standard program?

Plenty of situations still work; they just need the right product. A loan above the conforming limit is a jumbo loan. Condos have their own approval rules, a duplex can be financed as owner-occupied, and self-employed income is documented differently. A credit score you're unsure about isn't a dead end either; my post on what credit score you need in Wisconsin explains how guidelines vary by program and lender.

FAQ

Questions I hear most.

What is the most common home loan in Wisconsin?
Conventional loans are the most widely used, because they work for primary homes, second homes and investment property and their mortgage insurance can be removed later. That doesn't make them right for everyone. FHA, VA, USDA and WHEDA each solve a specific problem, such as thinner credit, military service, a rural address or limited savings, so I compare them before recommending one.
What are the 2026 loan limits in Wisconsin?
For 2026, the conforming loan limit for a one-unit home is $832,750 in every Wisconsin county, set by FHFA. The FHA limit for a one-unit home is $541,287 in most Wisconsin counties, including Milwaukee County, per HUD. Pierce and St. Croix counties in the Twin Cities metro are slightly higher. VA loans have no limit for borrowers with full entitlement.
Can I switch programs after I'm pre-approved?
Usually, yes. A pre-approval is built on your credit, income and assets, and most of that carries over if we move from FHA to conventional or add WHEDA assistance. Switching late in a transaction can affect timing, appraisal requirements or your agent's offer terms, so it's best to settle on a program before you write an offer.
Do I need to be a first-time buyer to get a low down payment loan?
No. Several programs with low down payment options, including FHA and Fannie Mae HomeReady, are open to repeat buyers. WHEDA's first-time pricing does require that you haven't owned a home in the past three years, with exceptions for federally designated target areas and eligible veterans. Credit guidelines vary by program and lender, so I'll confirm what fits you.
How fast can I close with these programs?
Timelines depend on the program, the appraisal and how quickly documents come back, but my team can close in as few as 9 to 18 days when everything lines up. Appraisals are often turned around in as little as three to four business days. Programs with extra steps, like WHEDA's homebuyer education, simply need those steps started early.
