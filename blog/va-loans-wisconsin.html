VA Loans in Wisconsin: Zero-Down Benefits for Veterans and Service Members

Ethan Brooks · Mortgage Advisor, NMLS #1639987 · 6 min read

If you've served, you have access to one of the strongest loan programs available anywhere — $0 down, no monthly mortgage insurance, and competitive rates. I still meet eligible veterans and active-duty buyers in Wisconsin who assume it's complicated, or that they don't qualify. Most of the time, they do. Here's how the VA loan actually works.

Who actually qualifies for a VA loan in Wisconsin?

Eligibility comes down to service history, not where you live. You'll generally qualify with 90 consecutive days of active service during wartime, 181 days during peacetime, or six years of service in the National Guard or Reserves — plus certain surviving spouses of veterans. You'll need a Certificate of Eligibility (COE) to confirm it, which a lender can usually pull in minutes using your DD-214 or service record, no separate application to the VA required on your end.

One thing that trips people up: this isn't a Wisconsin-specific program, and it isn't limited to buyers near a base. Any eligible veteran or service member buying a primary residence anywhere in the state can use it, whether that's a starter home in Green Bay or a lake property up north (as long as it's your primary residence, not a second home or rental).

What does "zero down" really mean here?

It means exactly what it sounds like — for eligible borrowers, VA loans allow 100% financing with no down payment required, on top of no monthly private mortgage insurance regardless of how little you put down. That combination is rare. Conventional loans charge PMI below 20% down, and FHA charges mortgage insurance premiums for the life of the loan in most cases. VA skips both.

That doesn't mean there are no upfront costs at all — you'll still have standard closing costs, and Wisconsin sellers can (and often do) agree to cover some of those as part of negotiations, which combined with $0 down can mean showing up to closing with very little cash.

What's the VA funding fee, and can you avoid it?

Instead of monthly mortgage insurance, VA loans charge a one-time funding fee, usually rolled into the loan amount rather than paid out of pocket. The exact percentage depends on your down payment and whether it's your first time using the benefit — first-time use with $0 down runs roughly in the low-2% range of the loan amount, dropping for larger down payments and rising slightly on subsequent uses. Veterans with a service-connected disability rating are exempt from the funding fee entirely, which is a detail some buyers don't realize applies to them.

Real numbers: Say you're buying a $290,000 home in Wisconsin with a VA loan, $0 down, first-time use. At roughly 2.15% (check the current published rate before locking in a number), the funding fee comes to about $6,235, financed into the loan rather than paid at closing. Compare that to an FHA purchase at the same price: 3.5% down means $10,150 in cash upfront, plus an upfront mortgage insurance premium and a monthly MIP payment that doesn't go away until you refinance or sell. The VA path typically costs less overall and requires far less cash to close.

How does VA compare to USDA and WHEDA options in Wisconsin?

VA and USDA loans are the only two zero-down programs widely available, but they serve different buyers — USDA is tied to eligible rural-designated addresses and income limits, while VA has no geographic restriction and no income cap at all. If you've served and qualify for VA, it's usually the stronger option even in an eligible USDA area, since VA skips the USDA's income ceiling and annual fee structure. Wisconsin's WHEDA down payment assistance programs are worth a look too, but they're generally most useful for buyers who don't have a military service connection.

The bottom line

If you've served and haven't looked into your VA loan benefit, it's worth ten minutes to check. There's no cost to pulling your Certificate of Eligibility, and knowing your number changes how you shop — you may be able to buy sooner, and with more cash left in your pocket, than you assumed.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a perfect credit score to get a VA loan?

No. The VA itself doesn't set a minimum credit score — that's up to the individual lender. Most lenders look for a score in the low-to-mid 600s, though some flexibility exists depending on the rest of your file, like residual income and payment history. It's generally more forgiving than conventional financing.

Can I use a VA loan more than once?

Yes. VA eligibility isn't a one-time benefit. As long as any prior VA loan has been paid off or you have remaining entitlement, you can use it again — whether that's buying your next home after a move or refinancing. Many career military families use it multiple times over their lives.

Is there a maximum VA loan amount in Wisconsin?

There's no cap set by the VA itself for borrowers with full entitlement — the limit comes down to what you qualify for based on income, debt, and credit. Borrowers with reduced entitlement (from a prior VA loan that hasn't been restored) may still see county-based limits apply, which a lender can check for your specific situation.
