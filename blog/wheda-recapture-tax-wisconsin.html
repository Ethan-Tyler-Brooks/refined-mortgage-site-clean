WHEDA Recapture Tax in Wisconsin: Will You Owe It?

Ethan Brooks · Mortgage Advisor, NMLS #1639987 · 7 min read

Somewhere in a WHEDA closing package is a form acknowledging a federal recapture tax. It is the most alarming piece of paper a first-time buyer signs, and almost nobody explains it first. Years later, when selling comes up, the memory surfaces as vague dread: don’t I owe the government money if I sell too soon?

The honest answer: most WHEDA sellers owe nothing, and the ones who do have a written promise from WHEDA to pay it back. Here is how the rule works, straight from WHEDA’s own homeowner guidance.

What is the federal recapture tax?

It has nothing to do with down payment assistance, which is where most of the confusion starts. Recapture is a federal tax rule attached to mortgages funded with tax-exempt bonds. A state housing authority sells those bonds to fund below-market home loans, which means the federal government is effectively subsidising the borrower. If that borrower sells quickly at a profit and their income has climbed well past where it was at purchase, the rule lets the government recover part of the subsidy.

WHEDA describes it plainly as paying back the federal government for the benefit of a lower interest mortgage loan funded by tax-exempt bonds. It is a tax on the sale, not a clawback of assistance, and it is owed to the IRS rather than to WHEDA.

Which WHEDA borrowers does it apply to?

Not all of them. WHEDA identifies three groups whose loans can carry recapture: borrowers financed through its first-time home buyer program, borrowers who used the VALOR program for veterans, and borrowers who were issued a Mortgage Credit Certificate at the time they bought alongside a WHEDA Advantage conventional loan.

If your loan is a plain conventional, FHA or VA loan with no WHEDA bond financing and no Mortgage Credit Certificate behind it, recapture is not your problem at all. One wrinkle: if you hold a Mortgage Credit Certificate but your first mortgage is not a WHEDA first mortgage, WHEDA says its reimbursement does not reach you — the federal rule still applies, the backstop does not. Your loan program is named in your closing documents, and the WHEDA loan programs we work with in Wisconsin will usually settle which bucket you are in.

When does recapture actually come due?

This is the part worth memorising, because it is much narrower than the paperwork suggests. WHEDA states that recapture applies only when all three of the following occur:

  1. You sell the home within the first nine full years of ownership.
  2. You realize a capital gain on the sale.
  3. Your income has risen above the federal allowable limits at the time of sale.

Miss any one of the three and no recapture tax is due. Sell in year ten and you are out. Sell at a loss, or break even after costs, and there is no gain to recapture against. Keep earning roughly what you earned when you bought, and the income test fails. WHEDA publishes the federal allowable income limits for Wisconsin on its own site, listed year by year, because the threshold changes with which year of ownership you sell in. There is also a clean exception: no recapture tax is due when the home is disposed of because of the homeowner’s death.

What does this look like on a real Wisconsin sale?

An example: a Waukesha buyer uses a WHEDA first-time buyer loan, sells in year five, and clears a capital gain of $26,000 after costs. Two of the three conditions are met. Whether anything is owed now turns entirely on the third: if household income is still under the federal limit published for year five of ownership, the answer is zero. If income has climbed past it, Form 8828 produces a number, the seller pays the IRS, and then asks WHEDA to reimburse it.

Does WHEDA pay the recapture tax for you?

For most current borrowers, yes, and this is the detail that gets lost. WHEDA offers a Recapture Tax Guaranty, acknowledged at closing on WHEDA Form 4. For WHEDA loans funded on or after April 1, 2013 that are subject to a federal recapture tax, WHEDA states it will reimburse the home buyer for the actual amount of recapture tax paid to the IRS when they sell their WHEDA-financed home.

Note the order of operations, because it trips people up. You pay the IRS first, with your return. Then you request reimbursement using WHEDA’s Exhibit 7 form, and WHEDA requires that request within six months of the date you filed the return that incurred the liability. Miss that window and you have given up money you were owed. Put the deadline in a calendar the day you file.

What do you still have to do at tax time?

File the form, even if you owe nothing. WHEDA is explicit that whether or not recapture tax is due, borrowers must file IRS Form 8828, Recapture of Federal Mortgage Subsidy, with their federal return for the year the home was sold. The form itself is what tests the three conditions and calculates the amount if there is one; IRS Publication 523, on selling your home, is the companion reading.

Practically, that means telling whoever prepares your return that the home was WHEDA financed. A preparer cannot infer it from a settlement statement. WHEDA recommends consulting a tax professional to determine whether the tax applies, and that is the right call — this is a tax question, not a mortgage question, and I am not your tax advisor.

The bottom line

Recapture is a real federal rule and a badly explained one. It reaches only bond-financed WHEDA loans and Mortgage Credit Certificates, it requires an early sale and a gain and a meaningful income increase, and WHEDA has promised since April 2013 to reimburse the tax when it does land. Nobody should pass up WHEDA down payment assistance out of fear of this form. Just keep your closing package, and read it again before you list.

Frequently asked questions

Do WHEDA borrowers have to pay the federal recapture tax?

Usually not, for two separate reasons. Most sellers fail at least one of the three conditions the federal rule requires, so no tax is due in the first place. And WHEDA publishes a Recapture Tax Guaranty: for WHEDA loans funded on or after April 1, 2013 that are subject to a federal recapture tax, WHEDA says it will reimburse the borrower for the actual amount of recapture tax paid to the IRS. The borrower pays the IRS first and then asks WHEDA for the money back.

When does the recapture tax apply after I sell my Wisconsin home?

Only when all three of these happen together: you sell within the first nine full years of ownership, you realize a capital gain on the sale, and your income has risen above the federal allowable limits for Wisconsin at the time of sale. Miss any one of the three and no recapture tax is due. There is also no recapture tax when the home is sold because the homeowner died.

Do I still have to file IRS Form 8828 if I do not owe anything?

Yes. WHEDA is explicit that whether or not recapture tax is due, borrowers must file IRS Form 8828 with their federal income tax return for the year the home is sold. The form is how the three conditions get tested and how the amount, if there is one, gets calculated. Tell whoever prepares your return that the home was WHEDA financed, because they cannot see it on a closing statement.
