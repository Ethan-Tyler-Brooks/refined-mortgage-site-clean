Wisconsin Conforming Loan Limit 2027: Where Jumbo Starts

Ethan Brooks · Mortgage Advisor, NMLS #1639987 · 6 min read

On November 24 the Federal Housing Finance Agency published the conforming loan limits for 2027. For a one-unit home anywhere in Wisconsin, the new number is $855,400. Above that, you are in jumbo territory — a different set of rules, not just a bigger loan.

This is one of the few mortgage numbers that changes on a fixed schedule every year, and one of the few worth knowing by heart if you are shopping at the top of a Wisconsin market. Here is what it governs, who it actually affects, and the timing question nobody thinks to ask.

What is the 2027 limit in Wisconsin?

For a one-unit property, $855,400. That applies in all 72 counties, with no local variation. Wisconsin has no county that FHFA designates as a high-cost area, so Waukesha, Dane, St. Croix and Door all work from the same ceiling as Rusk County does.

The 2026 limit was $832,750, so the ceiling moved up by $22,650. The multi-unit limits moved too:

  • One unit — $855,400 (was $832,750)
  • Two units — $1,095,250 (was $1,066,250)
  • Three units — $1,323,900
  • Four units — $1,645,350

Those figures come straight from FHFA’s published county-level limit file for 2027, not from an estimate.

Why does this one number matter so much?

Because it is the line between two different lending systems. At or under the limit, your loan is conforming: it meets Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac guidelines, which are public, standardized and the same at every lender in the country. Over the limit, the loan is a jumbo, and it is underwritten by whoever is willing to keep it. Those investors set their own guidelines, and they are generally stricter about reserves, documentation of income, and property types like condominiums.

So crossing the line does not simply mean borrowing more. It can mean a different documentation list, a different appraisal requirement, and a different answer to a question you already thought was settled.

A concrete case. You are buying in Brookfield and the loan amount works out to $900,000. That is $44,600 over the 2027 conforming ceiling, so it is a jumbo loan — even though the gap is small relative to the loan. Trim the loan amount to $855,400 or below and the same purchase runs through the conforming system instead. That is frequently a conversation worth having before you write the offer, because the structure of the deal is what moves you across the line, not the price of the house alone.

Who in Wisconsin actually runs into this?

Most buyers in this state never get near $855,400 in loan amount, and that is worth saying plainly. The buyers who do tend to fall into a few groups:

  • The high end of the Milwaukee suburbs. Brookfield, Elm Grove, Mequon, Fox Point and the Lake Country communities west of Waukesha all have inventory that gets there.
  • Lakefront and recreational property. A waterfront parcel in Walworth, Vilas or Door County can price well past what the surrounding county median would suggest.
  • New construction. A build that starts near the limit and finishes above it after change orders is a familiar story, and the limit is checked against the final loan amount.
  • Multi-unit buyers. This is the Wisconsin-specific one. The two-unit limit is $1,095,250, far above the one-unit figure, and in a city with as many duplexes as Milwaukee that difference decides what stays conforming.

When does the new limit apply to my file?

This is the part that catches people, and it is a fair question to put to any lender. FHFA’s limits apply to loans acquired by Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac during the calendar year — not to the date you signed an offer, and not strictly to your closing date either. Because a loan closing in late December is typically delivered to the agencies in January, many lenders start applying the following year’s higher limit well before January 1.

If your loan amount lands in the band between $832,750 and $855,400, that timing is the whole ballgame. The same file can be conforming or jumbo depending on when it is delivered, so ask the question early and get a direct answer instead of discovering the answer in underwriting. For the rules on the other side of the line, see jumbo loans in Wisconsin, and if you are weighing a two-unit, buying a duplex in Wisconsin covers how lenders treat the rent.

The bottom line

$855,400 is the 2027 conforming ceiling for a one-unit home everywhere in Wisconsin, up from $832,750. It is a limit on the loan, not the price. Crossing it changes the rulebook rather than just the size of the mortgage, and the date your loan is delivered can decide which rulebook you get. If you are shopping anywhere near that number, work out which side of the line your loan amount lands on before you write, not after.

Frequently asked questions

What is the conforming loan limit in Wisconsin for 2027?

For a one-unit home it is $855,400, and that figure applies in all 72 Wisconsin counties. FHFA announced it on November 24, 2026, and it replaces the 2026 limit of $832,750. Wisconsin has no county designated as a high-cost area, so unlike buyers in parts of California or Colorado, a buyer in Milwaukee County and a buyer in Bayfield County work from exactly the same number. The limits for small multi-unit property are higher: $1,095,250 for two units, $1,323,900 for three and $1,645,350 for four.

Does the loan limit apply to the purchase price or the loan amount?

The loan amount. This is the single most common mix-up, and it matters because it means a house priced above $855,400 can still be financed with a conforming loan if the money you actually borrow lands under the limit. The limit is a ceiling on the mortgage Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac will buy, not a ceiling on what the property can be worth or what you are allowed to pay for it.

When can I use the 2027 limit instead of the 2026 one?

The 2027 limits govern loans acquired by Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac during calendar year 2027, which is not the same thing as the date you write your offer. In practice many lenders begin applying the new, higher limit to loans closing in late December, because those loans are delivered in the new year. It is worth asking your lender directly rather than assuming, especially if your loan amount sits in the narrow band between the old limit and the new one.
