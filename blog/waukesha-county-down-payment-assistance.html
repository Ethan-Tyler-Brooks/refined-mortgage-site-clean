Waukesha County Down Payment Assistance: Who Qualifies

Ethan Brooks · Mortgage Advisor, NMLS #1639987 · 6 min read

Most Waukesha County buyers who ask me about down payment assistance are asking about WHEDA. There is a second program sitting right underneath it, run by the county itself, and the thing that disqualifies people most often is not their income or their credit. It is the price of the house.

If you are planning a spring purchase in the Milwaukee suburbs, sort this out in November rather than March. Every gate in it — the counseling certificate, the funding reservation, the county’s own inspection — runs on somebody else’s calendar.

Who actually runs down payment assistance in Waukesha County?

Not the City of Waukesha, and that is the first thing worth getting straight. Waukesha County is the lead agent for the HOME Consortium, a partnership formed in 1998 that now spans Jefferson, Ozaukee, Washington and Waukesha counties. It receives roughly $1.3 million a year from HUD, and a twelve-member board with equal representation from each county decides how that money is split.

So the service area is wider than the county name suggests. Nearly every municipality in all four counties has adopted a resolution to participate. The exceptions are easy to miss: the Towns of Eagle and Ottawa, and the Villages of Chenequa, Lac La Belle and Oconomowoc Lake. A buyer in Jefferson or Grafton can be eligible while a buyer two miles into a non-participating town is not.

The second thing to get straight is that you do not apply for it yourself. The county requires that eligible buyers reserve funds through a participating lender, and the lender receives the loan commitment from the Consortium’s program administrator. Your lender has to already be enrolled with the program. If they are not, the money is not available to you on that file at all — which is a question to ask at pre-approval, not at offer.

Is there a price limit on the house?

Yes, and in this market it is the rule that ends most conversations. HUD sets a maximum house value for each county in the Consortium, with a separate, higher ceiling for new construction, and the county updates them as HUD republishes them. As published, the ceiling for an existing home in Waukesha County is $390,000, with new construction allowed up to $440,000. The other three counties sit lower: Ozaukee and Washington in the mid-to-upper $300,000s for existing homes, and Jefferson lower still.

Read that against what is listed in Brookfield, Pewaukee or Elm Grove and the problem is obvious. The program is pointed at the lower half of the Waukesha County market, and plenty of buyers who meet every income and credit rule cannot find a qualifying house in the suburb they had in mind.

Put a number on it. A buyer looking at a $365,000 existing ranch in Pewaukee is under the published Waukesha County ceiling and can pursue the program. Move that buyer to a $410,000 house in Brookfield and the assistance is off the table — not because of anything about the buyer, but because the house is over the limit. New construction is the exception: at $410,000 a newly built home still fits. Confirm the figures with county staff, because HUD moves them.

Can my housing payment be too small to qualify?

It can, and almost nobody knows this. The program sets a minimum housing debt-to-income ratio of 18% alongside the usual maximums — household income at or below 80% of the area county median, and a total household debt-to-income ratio no higher than 50%.

A floor sounds backwards until you remember what the money is for. HOME dollars exist to help households that need help to buy. If your housing cost would be a small enough share of your income that you clearly do not need assistance, you fall out the bottom of the program rather than the top. In practice it catches the buyer purchasing well below what they could afford.

It is one of the few eligibility tests that gets harder as the house gets cheaper. If you are near either edge, have your lender run the ratios before you commit.

What has to happen before closing?

Two gates, and both take real time:

  • Homebuyer counseling. Every buyer receiving a down payment assistance loan has to complete a HOME Consortium homebuyer counseling program before the funds are released. Classes have schedules and the certificate is a hard gate. It is the easiest thing to finish early and the most common thing left until closing week.
  • The county’s code inspection. Every home has to pass the Consortium’s code requirements inspection before closing. This is separate from your own home inspection and separate from the appraisal — a third visit that can produce a repair list to negotiate with a seller who thought the inspection period had closed. On the older housing stock in Waukesha, Oconomowoc or West Bend, that matters.

Add the funding reservation and the sequence does not compress well. Write a closing date with slack in it. The bottom line

The program is worth the paperwork if the house fits. Check three things before you fall in love with a listing: whether the municipality participates, whether the price lands under the HUD ceiling for that county, and whether your housing ratio clears the floor as well as the ceiling. Then get the counseling certificate done and pick a lender already enrolled with the Consortium. Limits reset on HUD’s cycle, so confirm current terms with county staff.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to live in the City of Waukesha to qualify?

No, and this is the most common misunderstanding about the program. Waukesha County is the lead agent for the HOME Consortium, but the Consortium covers Jefferson, Ozaukee, Washington and Waukesha counties, and nearly every municipality in all four has formally joined it. A handful have not: the Towns of Eagle and Ottawa and the Villages of Chenequa, Lac La Belle and Oconomowoc Lake sit outside it. So a buyer in Hartford or Cedarburg may be eligible while a buyer inside Waukesha County is not, purely because of which municipality the parcel sits in. Confirm the specific address with county staff before you plan around the program.

Does Waukesha County down payment assistance have to be paid back?

Usually not, if you stay put. The assistance is recorded as a five-year forgivable loan, and a fifth of it is forgiven for each full year the home remains your primary residence, so after five full years nothing is owed. A partial repayment is triggered if you sell, transfer title, stop using the home as your primary residence, or take a cash-out refinance before the five years are up. That makes it a soft second mortgage rather than a grant, and it is worth knowing before you plan a move or a refinance inside that window.

Can I combine Waukesha County assistance with a WHEDA loan?

Yes. The county states that the HOME Consortium forgivable loan may be combined with other down payment assistance available through the state, the Federal Home Loan Bank, HUD, or personal and non-personal sources. In practice, layering has to be arranged in advance so that each program agrees to its position on the file, and your lender has to be enrolled with the Consortium to reserve the funds at all. Ask both questions during pre-approval rather than after you are under contract.
