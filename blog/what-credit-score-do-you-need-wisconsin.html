What Credit Score Do You Need to Buy a House in Wisconsin?

Ethan Brooks · Mortgage Advisor, NMLS #1639987 · 6 min read

Ask five people what credit score you need to buy a house and you'll get five different answers — most of them too high. The real minimum is lower than the number stuck in most buyers' heads, but the score you're actually aiming for depends on which loan program fits your situation.

I talk to buyers every week who assume they're years away from qualifying because they've heard you need a 740 or better. Some of them could be pre-approved this month. The minimum score gets you in the door; what happens after that is really a question of rate and cost, not eligibility. Let's separate the two.

What's the actual minimum, loan by loan?

There's no single "you need this score" answer, because different loan programs set different floors:

  • Conventional loans — typically require at least 620.
  • FHA loans — as low as 580 for the standard 3.5% down payment, or 500-579 with 10% down.
  • VA loans — there's no government-set minimum, but most lenders apply their own overlay, usually somewhere around 580-620.
  • USDA loans — generally look for 640 or better for automated approval, though manual underwriting can sometimes work with less.

Individual lenders can and do set their own minimums above these floors, which is why it's worth checking with more than one if your score is on the lower end. A "no" from one lender isn't always a "no" everywhere.

Does a higher score just help you qualify, or does it actually save you money?

Both — but the money side is where most people underestimate the difference. Once you clear a loan's minimum score, lenders don't treat every qualifying buyer the same. Loans are priced in tiers based on your credit score and down payment combined, and that tier decides your rate and how much mortgage insurance costs each month.

A real example: say two buyers each take out a $300,000, 30-year fixed loan. Rates move daily and aren't guaranteed, but illustratively, a buyer with a 620 score might land around 7.125%, putting principal and interest near $2,021 a month. A buyer with a 760 score on the same loan amount might land closer to 6.375%, near $1,872 a month. That's roughly $150 a month — about $1,795 a year, and nearly $9,000 over five years — before even counting the lower mortgage insurance a higher score typically brings. Same house, same loan amount, different score.

What if your score isn't where you want it yet?

A lower score today doesn't have to mean a delayed purchase. Some of the fastest, most reliable moves I see buyers make in the months before applying:

  • Pay down revolving balances (credit cards) below about 30% of their limit — this often has the single biggest short-term impact.
  • Check your reports for errors and dispute anything inaccurate; mistakes are more common than people expect.
  • Avoid opening new credit accounts or financing furniture/appliances right before applying.
  • Ask a family member with strong credit about adding you as an authorized user on an older account.
  • Ask your lender about a rapid rescore once balances are paid down — it can update your score in days rather than a full billing cycle.

Does one late payment sink your approval?

Usually not. Underwriters look at the overall pattern, not a single data point. A late payment from three years ago carries far less weight than a recent 30-day late or a string of them. What tends to raise real concern is recent, repeated, or large delinquencies — not an isolated slip years back. If something like that is sitting on your report, it's worth discussing directly rather than guessing at how it'll be read.

The bottom line

The minimum credit score to buy a house in Wisconsin is probably lower than you've assumed — but the score that gets you the best rate is a separate conversation worth having before you assume anything about your timeline. Pulling your credit and reviewing your actual numbers takes about fifteen minutes and tells you far more than any online rule of thumb.

Frequently asked questions

What credit score do you need to buy a house in Wisconsin?

It depends on the loan. Conventional loans typically require at least 620. FHA loans allow as low as 580 with 3.5% down (or 500-579 with 10% down). VA loans have no set government minimum, though most lenders set their own around 580-620. USDA loans usually look for 640 or better.

Can you buy a house with a 580 credit score?

Yes, in many cases. FHA loans are built for exactly this — 580 qualifies for the standard 3.5% down payment. Some lenders set higher internal minimums, so it's worth checking with more than one if your score is in that range.

Does your credit score affect your mortgage rate?

Significantly. Lenders price loans in tiers based on credit score and down payment. A higher score usually means a lower rate and lower mortgage insurance cost, even if both buyers qualify for the same loan.
