Does My Spouse's Debt Count If I Buy Alone in Wisconsin?

Ethan Brooks · Mortgage Advisor, NMLS #1639987 · 7 min read

Plenty of Wisconsin couples decide that only one of them should be on the mortgage, usually because one has the stronger credit file. The assumption that follows is the one that costs people their pre-approval: that leaving a spouse off the loan also leaves their debts out of the math. In Wisconsin that depends entirely on which loan program you use, and the difference is big enough to change what you can buy.

Why does Wisconsin treat a spouse's debt differently?

Because of how Wisconsin owns property. The Marital Property Act moved Wisconsin from a common-law property system to a community property system, and the Department of Revenue states it plainly in Publication 109: Wisconsin is one of nine community property states, alongside Arizona, California, Idaho, Louisiana, Nevada, New Mexico, Texas and Washington.

Most of the time that is a tax and estate question. In lending it matters because some federal loan programs keep a separate rulebook for community property states. A buyer doing the same thing in Illinois or Minnesota gets a different answer, and almost nobody expects that going in.

Does FHA count a non-borrowing spouse's debt in Wisconsin?

Yes. HUD's underwriting handbook has a section titled Non-Borrowing Spouse Debt in Community Property States, and it leaves no room to negotiate. If the borrower lives in a community property state, or the property being insured is in one, the debts of the non-borrowing spouse must be included in the borrower's qualifying ratios, except for obligations specifically excluded by state law.

In practice that means three things happen on an FHA file in Wisconsin even when your spouse signs nothing:

  • A credit report is pulled on them. HUD requires it, precisely so the underwriter can see which debts have to be counted.
  • Their monthly obligations land in your debt-to-income ratio. Car loans, student loans, credit card minimums, personal loans — the same items that would count if they were applying with you.
  • Judgments and collections get looked at. A court judgment against a non-borrowing spouse generally has to be resolved or paid, and collection accounts get folded into the total the underwriter has to review.

There is one important piece of protection in the same rule, and buyers are rarely told about it: the non-borrowing spouse's credit history is not a reason to deny the application. Their score is not blended with yours, it does not set your pricing, and a rough payment history on their report is not grounds for a decline. It is their debt that follows you into the file, not their credit reputation.

What does this look like in real dollars?

Take a Wauwatosa couple. You are applying alone because your credit file is the better one. Your spouse is not on the loan, and their report shows a $480 car payment and $210 in student loan payments.

On a conventional loan, those two lines are irrelevant. Nobody asks about them. On an FHA loan in Wisconsin, $690 a month gets added to the debt side of your ratio before the underwriter looks at a single thing about the house. That is $690 of monthly capacity that is no longer available to carry a mortgage payment, property taxes and insurance — and in a state where escrowed property taxes are already a heavy line, it tightens the file considerably.

This is why program choice comes before house hunting. The same buyer, the same spouse, the same debts, can qualify comfortably on one program and get declined on another — purely because of which rulebook applies. Does a conventional loan work the same way?

No, and this is the lever most Wisconsin buyers in this position end up pulling. Conventional loans are underwritten to Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac guidelines, which consider the borrowers on the application and nobody else. Leave a spouse off, and their debts stay off your ratio entirely. No credit report on them, no car loan, no student loans.

That can flip the usual advice on its head. FHA is often the easier approval on credit. But when the obstacle is a spouse carrying real debt, the community property rule can make conventional the only program where the numbers work.

Keep one thing separate from all of this: whether your spouse has to sign at closing is a different question, and the answer is usually yes regardless of whose income got you approved. What should you do before you apply?

The useful work here happens before anyone pulls credit.

  • Add up your spouse's monthly obligations. Not the balances — the monthly payments, because that is what hits the ratio. Pull their report together rather than guessing.
  • Ask your lender to run both programs. Any lender should show you the FHA version with the spousal debt counted and the conventional version without it. If you only get one, ask for the other.
  • Check for judgments and collections early. These turn into a closing-day scramble on an FHA file, and they take time to clear.
  • Do not pay anything down on a guess. Which debts move the needle depends on the program and where your ratio sits.

The bottom line

In Wisconsin, leaving your spouse off the mortgage does not automatically leave their debt out of it. On an FHA loan it comes with you, because Wisconsin is a community property state and HUD says so. On a conventional loan it does not. Their credit score never counts against you either way. Knowing which of those two rulebooks your file will be read under, before you write an offer, is the difference between a pre-approval that holds up and one that quietly shrinks at underwriting.

Frequently asked questions

Does my spouse's debt count on a Wisconsin mortgage if they are not on the loan?

On an FHA loan, yes. Wisconsin is a community property state, and HUD requires a lender to include a non-borrowing spouse's debts in your qualifying ratios unless state law specifically excludes the obligation. On a conventional loan, no. Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac underwrite only the people who signed the application, so a spouse left off the loan is left out of the ratios too.

Can my spouse's bad credit stop my FHA loan in Wisconsin?

Not on its own. HUD is explicit that a non-borrowing spouse's credit history is not a reason to deny the application, and their score is never blended into yours or used to price the loan. What does carry over is the debt on their report, plus two specific items: a court judgment generally has to be resolved or paid, and collection accounts get added to the total the underwriter reviews.

Does a conventional loan count my spouse's debt in Wisconsin?

No. Conventional underwriting looks at the borrowers on the application and nobody else, so leaving a spouse off keeps their car loan, student loans and credit cards out of your debt-to-income ratio. That is the main reason a Wisconsin buyer with a high-debt spouse often lands on conventional financing rather than FHA, even when FHA would otherwise be the easier approval.
