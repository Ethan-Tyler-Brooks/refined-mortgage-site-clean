What Happens If Your Home Loses Value After You Close?

Ethan Brooks · Mortgage Advisor, NMLS #1639987 · 6 min read

If your home is worth less than you paid, your mortgage doesn't change. Your rate, your payment and your loan terms stay exactly where they were at closing. What a lower value does affect is your equity, and with it your options to drop PMI, refinance, borrow against the home or sell without bringing cash.

I get this question from buyers who closed at the top of a bidding war and then watched a neighbor's house sell for less. It's an uncomfortable feeling, but it's usually a paper loss, not a real one, unless you need to sell or borrow soon. Here's what actually changes and what you can do about it.

Can home values actually go down?

Yes. Values move with interest rates, local supply, the job market and the condition of the house itself. Southeastern Wisconsin has historically been steadier than the boom-and-bust coastal markets, but individual neighborhoods and individual homes can still dip, especially after a period of heavy competition. A short dip matters far less than people think if you plan to stay put.

Does my mortgage payment or rate change if my home loses value?

No. Your lender can't raise your rate, change your payment or call your loan because the market moved. A fixed-rate mortgage stays fixed. As long as you make your payments, pay your property taxes and keep the home insured, the loan simply keeps amortizing.

One thing that can move is your escrow. If your municipality lowers your assessment, your property tax bill may drop, and your lender will adjust the escrow portion of your payment at the next annual escrow analysis. More on that in my guide to Wisconsin property taxes and escrow.

What does a lower value change?

  • Your equity. Equity is value minus what you owe. A lower value means less equity on paper, and in a steep drop you can owe more than the home is worth (being "underwater").
  • Removing PMI early. Under the federal Homeowners Protection Act, you can ask to cancel PMI once your balance reaches the scheduled point based on your original value. Your servicer may require proof the value hasn't fallen below that original value, so a dip can delay an early request. The automatic cut-off at the later scheduled point still happens regardless of value. See how to get rid of PMI.
  • Borrowing against the home. A home equity loan or HELOC depends on today's value, so less equity means a smaller line or none at all. If you already have a HELOC, federal rules let the lender freeze or reduce it if the value drops significantly.

Can I refinance if my home value dropped?

Often, yes. It depends on your loan type:

  • FHA streamline refinance: generally no new appraisal, so a lower value usually doesn't block it.
  • VA IRRRL: also designed to work without a new appraisal in most cases.
  • Conventional refinance: uses a new appraisal or valuation, so less equity can mean mortgage insurance or a different program.

Whether a refinance is worth it comes down to the break-even math, which I walk through in when refinancing is worth it and on my Wisconsin refinance page.

What if I need to sell while values are down?

If you sell for more than you owe plus selling costs, you walk away with less than you hoped, but you walk away clean. If you owe more than the sale would bring, you'd need to bring cash to closing, negotiate a short sale with your servicer, or wait. If you can wait, waiting is usually the cheapest option, because time and regular payments rebuild equity from both directions.

If you're thinking about moving up rather than out, it can still work. A lower value on your current home often comes with softer prices on the next one. See how to buy before you sell.

Can I lower my Wisconsin property taxes if my home lost value?

Maybe. Wisconsin assesses property as of January 1 each year. If your assessment is higher than what your home would realistically sell for, start by talking to your municipal assessor during Open Book, the informal review period. If that doesn't resolve it, you can file a formal objection with your municipality's Board of Review. Deadlines are strict, so watch for your assessment notice and act quickly. Recent comparable sales and a copy of your purchase appraisal are your best evidence.

What should I do right now?

  • Keep making your payments. Your credit and your options depend on it.
  • Don't make decisions based on one online estimate. Automated values can be off, especially for older Milwaukee-area homes.
  • Check your assessment notice each spring.
  • If you have PMI, refinancing questions or plans to move in the next couple of years, call me and we'll look at the real numbers.

The bottom line

A lower home value doesn't change your mortgage. It changes your equity, which matters most if you want to drop PMI, refinance into a conventional loan, borrow against the home or sell soon. If you're staying put, time is on your side.

Frequently asked questions

Can my lender do anything if my home loses value?

No. As long as you make your payments and keep the home insured, a drop in market value doesn't let your lender change your rate, raise your payment or call the loan. Your mortgage is based on the balance you owe, not today's value.

Can I refinance if my home value dropped?

Often, yes. FHA streamline and VA IRRRL refinances usually don't require a new appraisal, so a lower value matters less. Conventional refinances use a new value, so a drop can limit your options or require mortgage insurance. I can check which path fits your loan.

Can I lower my Wisconsin property taxes if my home value fell?

Possibly. Wisconsin assesses property as of January 1. If your assessment is higher than what your home would sell for, you can talk to the assessor during Open Book and, if needed, file an objection with your municipality's Board of Review before its deadline.
